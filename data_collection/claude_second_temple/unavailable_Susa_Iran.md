- Bloch, Yigal, 2014. "Judeans in Sippar and Susa during the First Century of the Babylonian Exile: Assimilation and Perseverance under Neo-Babylonian and Achaemenid Rule." Journal of Ancient Near Eastern History 1(2), 119-172. — The primary edition and discussion of the Susa promissory notes of 494 and 493 BCE (texts nos. 7-8) naming Judeans with Yahwistic names at the Persian court; known here only through Alstola 2020 and the Maaravi article. — Paywall (De Gruyter); no open-access copy found.
- Schiffman, Lawrence H., 2018. "What We Know about Jews in Persia's Ancient Capital" (Mosaic Magazine pick of a Schiffman article on Shushan). https://mosaicmagazine.com/picks/2018/04/what-we-know-about-jews-in-persias-ancient-capital/ — Describes an Akkadian loan document from Shushan with two Jewish witnesses soon after the Persian conquest as evidence for an early Persian-period Jewish community. — Paywall/redirect to ideas.tikvah.org; only the search snippet seen; the original Schiffman article was not located.
- Zadok, Ran, 2002. The Earliest Diaspora: Israelites and Judeans in Pre-Hellenistic Mesopotamia. Tel Aviv: Diaspora Research Institute, Publication 151. — Prosopographic survey of Israelites/Judeans in cuneiform sources, likely including Susa and Elam. — Not online.
- Waerzeggers, Caroline, 2010. "Babylonians in Susa: The Travels of Babylonian Businessmen to Susa Reconsidered." In B. Jacobs and R. Rollinger (eds.), Der Achämenidenhof / The Achaemenid Court, 777-813. — Context for the Babylonian (and Judean) officials documented in Susa (494/493 BCE). — Not online (book chapter).
- Stolper, Matthew W., 1992. "The Murašû Texts from Susa." Revue d'Assyriologie 86, 69-77. — Cuneiform texts written at Susa in the reign of Darius II involving Nippur officials; could hold more West Semitic/Judean names at Susa. — Not online (paywall/print).
- Neusner, Jacob, 1965. A History of the Jews in Babylonia, I: The Parthian Period. Leiden: Brill. — Might address Jews in Elymais/Susiana in the Parthian period. — Not online.
- Encyclopaedia Iranica, "Susa" and "Judeo-Persian Communities" entries. — Could summarize Jewish evidence at Susa in antiquity. — Known blocker (not retried).
