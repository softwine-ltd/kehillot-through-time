# Unavailable sources: Tiberias, Israel (Second Temple period)

- Crossan, J. D. & Reed, J. L., 2001. *Excavating Jesus: Beneath the Stones, Behind the Texts.* HarperSanFrancisco, p. 71. — Cited by a Routledge chapter as source of the estimate of 8,000-12,000 inhabitants and 100-125 acres for Tiberias at its founding (c. 20 CE). — Book not online; the citing chapter returned 403.
- Anon./ed., "Tiberias" chapter, Routledge reference volume, DOI 10.4324/9780203087657-53 (https://www.taylorfrancis.com/chapters/edit/10.4324/9780203087657-53/tiberias). — Gives the 8,000-12,000 estimate and area, and the early gate (c. 20 CE). — 403 Forbidden (paywall); seen only as a search snippet.
- Reed, J. L., 2002. *Archaeology and the Galilean Jesus: A Re-examination of the Evidence.* Trinity Press International, pp. 82, 117. — Source of the 4,500-15,000 range cited on Wikipedia and of Reed's own population figures for Tiberias. — Book not online.
- Reed, J. L., 1994. "Population Numbers, Urbanization, and Economics: Galilean Archaeology and the Historical Jesus." SBL 1994 Seminar Papers, 203-219. — Likely contains Reed's area x density estimates for Tiberias and Sepphoris. — Only a catalogue record online (researchworks.laverne.edu).
- Meyers, E. M., 1997. "Jesus and His Galilean Context." In Edwards & McCollough (eds.), *Archaeology and the Galilee*, 57-66 (p. 59). — Estimate of 24,000 for Tiberias. — Not online; known only via Fiensy & Strange footnote.
- Overman, J. A., 1988. "Who Were the First Urban Christians?" SBL 1988 Seminar Papers, 160-168. — Estimate of 30,000-40,000 for Tiberias. — Not online; known via Fiensy & Strange footnote.
- Horsley, R. A., 1996. *Archaeology, History, and Society in Galilee.* Trinity Press International, p. 45. — Combined estimate of 15,000 for Sepphoris and Tiberias. — Not online.
- Chancey, M. A., 2002. *The Myth of a Gentile Galilee.* SNTSMS 118, Cambridge University Press, esp. pp. 79-99. — Full discussion of Jewish/gentile composition of first-century Tiberias (stone vessels, ossuaries, inscriptions). — Paywalled; only his 2003 Bible Interpretation summary was read.
- Qedar, S., 1986-87. "Two Lead Weights of Herod Antipas and Agrippa II and the Early History of Tiberias." *Israel Numismatic Journal* 9, 29-30. — First publication of the 30/31 CE agoranomos weight. — Not online.
- Jensen, M. H., 2006 (2nd ed. 2010). *Herod Antipas in Galilee.* WUNT II 215, Mohr Siebeck. — Assessment of Tiberias' foundation, size and population. — Paywalled / not online.
- Weiss, Z., 2022. "בין שתי ערים" (Between Two Cities). *Et-Mol* 280, 1-5. https://files.ybz.org.il/periodicals/Etmol/280/280.1.pdf — Discussion of the large Jewish population of Tiberias and Sepphoris; may contain size estimates. — PDF returned 403; only the abstract read.
- Miller, S. & Snyder, F., 2026. "The Life and Afterlife of the Palace of Herod Antipas in Tiberias." *BASOR* 395(1), 17-45. https://doi.org/10.1086/740331 — Early-Roman stratigraphy and chronology of Antipas's Tiberias. — 403 paywall; abstract only.
- Leibner, U. & Amitzur, Y., 2021. "מערת קבורה מפוארת מן התקופה הרומית בטבריה." *Qadmoniot* 54(162), 81-84. https://www.jstor.org/stable/27168458 — Roman-period burial cave with clay ossuaries and Greek name inscriptions near Tiberias; date and Jewish identification not given in abstract. — JSTOR paywall; abstract only.
- Hartal, M., 2002 excavation at Galei Kinneret Hotel (stadium, stone "measuring cups"), excavation report (Hadashot Arkheologiyot / IAA). — Context and dating of the stone vessels evidencing the 1st-2nd c. Jewish community. — Report not located online; known via NEAEHL entry.
- Mills, W. E. & Bullard, R. A. (eds.), 1998. *Mercer Dictionary of the Bible*, p. 917. — Cited by Wikipedia for the council of 600 with a committee of ten until 44 CE and for Antipas settling non-Jews. — Not online.
- Haaretz, 2009, article on the Tiberias theater (Hirschfeld & Stefanski). — Hirschfeld's statement that Tiberias was a Jewish city and the early-1st-c. theater date. — Original not checked; read only as quoted on BiblePlaces Blog.
