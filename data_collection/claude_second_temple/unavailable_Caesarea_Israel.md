# Unavailable sources: Caesarea, Israel (Second Temple period)

- Levine, Lee I., 1975. *Caesarea under Roman Rule*. Studies in Judaism in Late Antiquity 7. Leiden: Brill. — Standard monograph; chapter on the first century (Agrippa I, the Jewish-Greek conflict, the Revolt) and the Jewish community; may discuss size of the Jewish population and the 20,000 figure. — Not online (Google Books / library only).
- Levine, Lee I., 1974. "The Jewish-Greek Conflict in First Century Caesarea." *Journal of Jewish Studies* 25, 381-397. — Analysis of the civic-rights dispute 59-66 CE and the community's status/size. — Paywalled / not accessed.
- Levine, Lee I., 1975. *Roman Caesarea: An Archaeological-Topographical Study*. Qedem 2. Jerusalem: Hebrew University. — City area and topography relevant to any density-based population estimate. — Not online.
- Kasher, Aryeh, 1977. "The Isopoliteia Question in Caesarea Maritima." *Jewish Quarterly Review* 68, 16-27. — Legal status and standing of the Jewish community before 66 CE. — JSTOR paywall.
- Kasher, Aryeh, 1990. *Jews and Hellenistic Cities in Eretz-Israel* (TSAJ 21). Tübingen: Mohr Siebeck. — Chapters on Strato's Tower under the Hasmoneans and Caesarea 59-70 CE; Wikipedia cites Kasher for the 2,500 captives killed in 70 CE. — Not online.
- Roller, D. W. & Hohlfelder, R. L., 1983. "The Problem of the Location of Straton's Tower." *BASOR* 252. — Cited by Wikipedia for Jannaeus's capture (90 BCE) and Strato's Tower becoming a "Jewish village" for two generations. — JSTOR paywall; claim checked only via Wikipedia.
- Holum, K. G. & Raban, A., 1993. "Caesarea." In E. Stern (ed.), *New Encyclopedia of Archaeological Excavations in the Holy Land*, vol. 1. — Hasmonean Jewish population "probably", Herod resettled the site with Jews and pagans, 66 CE massacre of "most" Jews. — Print only; read only as quoted on deadseaquake.info.
- Patrich, Joseph, 2026. "A Roman-Period Jewish Peristyle House with a Miqweh at Caesarea Maritima." *'Atiqot* 121. https://doi.org/10.70967/2948-040X.2524 — Villa first built early 1st c. CE with a miqweh in its bathhouse; full text needed to tell whether the miqweh / Jewish ownership predates 70 CE. — Abstract only; PDF returned 403 / Cloudflare challenge.
- Patrich, Joseph, 2011. *Studies in the Archaeology and History of Caesarea Maritima: Caput Judaeae, Metropolis Palaestinae*. Leiden: Brill. — Herodian city, synagogue site, Jewish community. — Paywalled.
- Govaars, M., Spiro, M. & White, L. M., 2009. *Field O: The "Synagogue" Site* (Joint Expedition to Caesarea Maritima Excavation Reports 9). Boston: ASOR. — Possible Early Roman house-synagogue (stratum II) beneath the later synagogue; Avi-Yonah's 1956/1962 excavation. — Not online (only AJA review snippet seen in search).
- Ringel, Joseph, 1975. *Césarée de Palestine: étude historique et archéologique*. Paris. — History of the Jewish community in Hellenistic-Roman Caesarea. — Not online.
