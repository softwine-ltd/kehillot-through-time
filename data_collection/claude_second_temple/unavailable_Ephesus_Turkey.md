# Unavailable sources: Ephesus, Turkey

- Trebilco, Paul, 2004. "The Jewish Community in Ephesus" (ch. 1). The Early Christians in Ephesus from Paul to Ignatius (WUNT 166). Tübingen: Mohr Siebeck, pp. 37ff. https://cdn.mohrsiebeck.com/9b,0bf9d887ed4fe296d93583f2f49e73.pdf — The main modern study: covers how the community was founded, the decrees in Josephus and Philo, the synagogue, inscriptions and city-wide organization; may say how large the community was. — Not accessed: the publisher PDF returned 404, and the book is paywalled.
- Trebilco, Paul. "The Jewish Community in Ephesus and Its Interaction with Christ-Believers in the First Century CE and Beyond." Chapter (pp. 93-126) in an edited volume on JSTOR, https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt20d87z1 — Its abstract says the Josephus, Philo and inscription evidence shows a significant community that could act as a unified body in the first century CE. — Not accessed: the JSTOR page would not load (paywall).
- Trebilco, Paul R., 1991. Jewish Communities in Asia Minor (SNTSMS 69). Cambridge: Cambridge University Press. — The book version of the 1987 Durham thesis, which I did read. — Not accessed: paywalled.
- Ameling, Walter, 2004. Inscriptiones Judaicae Orientis II: Kleinasien (TSAJ 99). Tübingen: Mohr Siebeck. — Corpus of the Jewish inscriptions of Ephesus with dates (archiatros, priest M. Aur. Moussios, presbyters, menorah graffiti; most are probably after 74 CE). — Not accessed: paywalled, not online.
- Pucci Ben Zeev, Miriam, 1998. Jewish Rights in the Roman World: The Greek and Roman Documents Quoted by Josephus Flavius (TSAJ 74). Tübingen: Mohr Siebeck. — The standard commentary on dating the Ephesian decrees (Lentulus 49 BCE, Dolabella 43 BCE, Ephesian decree c. 42 BCE, Agrippa, Julius Antonius). — Not accessed: paywalled, not online.
- Schürer, E., rev. Vermes, Millar, Goodman, 1986. The History of the Jewish People in the Age of Jesus Christ, vol. III.1, pp. 22-23, 120. Edinburgh: T&T Clark. — Survey of the Jews of Ephesus and their Roman citizenship (cited by Trebilco 1987). — Not accessed: not online.
- Safrai, S. and Stern, M. (eds.), 1974. The Jewish People in the First Century (CRINT I.1), pp. 144, 152, 441. Assen: Van Gorcum. — Says of the Ephesian Jews that "Quite a number of them were Roman citizens"; dates the Ephesian decree (cited by Trebilco 1987). — Not accessed: not online.
- Smallwood, E. Mary, 1981. The Jews under Roman Rule, pp. 127-128, 142. Leiden: Brill. — Assessment of the Lentulus exemption ("infinitesimally small" number affected) and the date of the Julius Antonius letter (cited by Trebilco 1987). — Not accessed: not online / paywalled.
- Tcherikover, Victor, 1961. Hellenistic Civilization and the Jews, pp. 330, 342. Philadelphia: JPS. — "A considerable group of Jews" at Ephesus held Roman citizenship; doubts Josephus' Seleucid citizenship claims (cited by Trebilco 1987). — Not accessed: not online.
- Robert, Louis, 1960. Hellenica 11-12, pp. 381-384. — The Ephesian Jews' self-designation "hoi Ioudaioi" and the Jew Marcus Mussius at Ephesus (cited by Trebilco 1987). — Not accessed: not online.
- Hicks, E. L., 1890. The Collection of Ancient Greek Inscriptions in the British Museum III, no. 677; and Engelmann, H. & Knibbe, D., 1978-80, "Aus ephesischen Skizzenbüchern," JÖAI 52, p. 50 no. 94. — Jewish inscriptions from Ephesus (the Jewish archiatros, dated after Antoninus Pius, so outside the period). — Not accessed: not checked online.
- Anonymous/unknown author, c. 1900. Article on Ephesus and the Jews' exemption from military service. The Expositor, 6th series, vol. 5, p. 92ff. https://gospelstudies.org.uk/biblicalstudies/pdf/expositor/series6/05-092.pdf — Discussion of the Lentulus/Dolabella decrees. — Not accessed: the PDF is a scanned image with no text layer, and I did not run OCR on it.
