# Unavailable sources: Maresha, Israel

- Kloner, A. and Stern, I., 2007. "Idumea in the Late Persian Period (Fourth Century B.C.E.)." In O. Lipschits, G. N. Knoppers and R. Albertz (eds), Judah and the Judeans in the Fourth Century B.C.E., Eisenbrauns, pp. 139-144 (pp. 142-3 cited). — Source of the inference (cited by Shatzman 2013, n. 99) that Jews were perhaps ~10% of the population, from c. 1,300 personal names in Maresha/Idumean ostraca and inscriptions; concerns 4th-c. BCE Idumea; may give a breakdown of Judean (Yahwistic) names. — Not online (book chapter).
- Stern, I., 2007. "The Population of Persian-Period Idumea according to the Ostraca: A Study of Ethnic Boundaries and Ethnogenesis." In Y. Levin (ed.), A Time of Change: Judah and its Neighbours in the Persian and Early Hellenistic Periods, T&T Clark, pp. 205-238. — Onomastic analysis of Idumean ostraca incl. share of Judean/Yahwistic names. — Not online / paywalled.
- Kloner, A., 2011. "The Identity of the Idumeans Based on the Archaeological Evidence from Maresha." In O. Lipschits, G. N. Knoppers and M. Oeming (eds), Judah and the Judeans in the Achaemenid Period, Eisenbrauns, pp. 563-573. — Ethnic composition of Maresha, Judean component, Hasmonean conquest and abandonment. — Bibliographic entry only.
- Stern, I., 2019/2020. "The Evolution of an Edomite/Idumean Identity: Hellenistic Maresha as a Case Study." In B. Hensel et al. (eds), About Edom and Idumea in the Persian Period, Equinox. https://api.equinoxpub.com/chapter/show/42821 — Discusses Idumeans, Judeans and Arabs at Maresha up to Hyrcanus' conquest. — Abstract only (paywall; academia.edu copy not fetched).
- Stern, I., 2012. Article on circumcised phalli figurines from Maresha, Strata: Bulletin of the Anglo-Israel Archaeological Society 30. — Evidence on Idumean circumcision before/at the Hasmonean conversion. — Not online.
- Zissu, B. and Kloner, A. "Hellenistic Residences at Maresha (Marissa)." https://www.operaipogea.it/wp-content/uploads/1995/03/15-Zissu-Kloner-hellenistic-Maresha_compressed.pdf — Includes a section on population estimation for Hellenistic Maresha (total population of an Idumean city, relevant as context). — PDF is scanned images with no text layer; no OCR available.
- Zissu, B. and Kloner, A., 2025. "The Final Dwelling: Funerary Architecture and Burial Customs at Hellenistic-Period Maresha." Journal of Ancient History and Archaeology 12(2). https://jaha.org.ro/index.php/JAHA/article/view/1279 — Abstract says later reuse of kokhim and ossilegium indicate "Jewish presence in the Roman period" and civic activity until 108/7 BCE; full text needed to date the Jewish reuse. — PDF download 403/500.
- Anonymous (academia.edu), "Marisa Introduction." https://www.academia.edu/38337692/Marisa_Introduction — Gives c. 10,000 inhabitants in the Hellenistic period and conquest 112/1-108/7 BCE with possible small post-conquest military force. — 403.
- Shatzman, I., 2012. (Study of the date of the Hasmonean conquest of Marisa, cited as Shatzman 2012, 37-43 in Shatzman 2013.) — Detailed argument for conquest in 108/107 BCE and archaeological evidence for brief Hasmonean garrison. — Not located online.
- Kloner, A., 2003. Maresha Excavations Final Report I: Subterranean Complexes 21, 44, 70. IAA Reports 17, Jerusalem. — Excavation evidence for abandonment after the conquest and Gabinian/Beth Guvrin relocation. — Not online.
- Kloner, A., Eshel, E., Korzakova, H. B. and Finkielsztejn, G., 2010. Maresha Excavations Final Report III: Epigraphic Finds from the 1989-2000 Seasons. IAA Reports 45. — Ostraca and inscriptions with personal names (Judean names) and dated inscriptions down to 108/7 BCE. — Not online.
- Faust, A. and Erlich, A., 2011. The Excavations of Khirbet er-Rasm, Israel (BAR Int. Ser.). — Hasmonean conquest of Idumea in the Maresha region. — Not online.
- Weitzman, S., 1999. "Forced Circumcision and the Shifting Role of Gentiles in Hasmonean Ideology." HTR 92. — Read (footnotes visible); the original publication of the 1st-c. CE Jerusalem-style tomb at Marisa that it cites was not checked. — Original excavation report not identified/not online.
