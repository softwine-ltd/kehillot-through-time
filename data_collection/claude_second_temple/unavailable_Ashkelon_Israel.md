- Fuks, Gideon, 2001. Ashkelon, a City by the Sea / study of Hellenistic-Roman Ascalon (Hebrew; cited as "Fuks (2001)" by Wikipedia "Tel Ashkelon"). — Main monograph on Hellenistic-Roman Ascalon; likely discusses Jews in the city, its independence from the Hasmoneans, and the 66 CE massacre. — Not online.
- Safrai, Ze'ev, 2001. "Ashkelon - a Gentile Enclave: Ashkelon and the Coastal Cities after the Roman Conquest" (Hebrew). In: Ashkelon - A City by the Sea, pp. 89-97. https://cris.biu.ac.il/en/publications/ (Bar-Ilan record) — Likely discusses the Jewish minority in Roman Ashkelon. — Catalogue record only, no text.
- Kasher, Aryeh, 1990. Jews and Hellenistic Cities in Eretz-Israel. Tübingen: Mohr Siebeck (TSAJ 21). — Chronological study of Jewish relations with Ascalon and other Hellenistic cities to 70 CE; may hold evidence on Jewish residents and the 66 CE massacre. — Paywalled/book not online (only front matter PDF found).
- Birney, Kathleen J., 2022. Ashkelon 9: The Hellenistic Period. Eisenbrauns. — Final excavation report of the Leon Levy Expedition for the Hellenistic/early Roman city; would show any Jewish material culture (stone vessels, Hasmonean coins). — Not open access.
- Ciecieląg, Jerzy, 2011. "Ascalon Coins and the Roots of the Herodian Dynasty." Notae Numismaticae 6. https://digi.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/diglit/notae_numismaticae2011/0035 — Discusses the Ascalonite origin tradition of the Herodian family. — Access denied (bot protection).
- Encyclopaedia Judaica (2007), "Ashkelon" — the statement that a Jewish community flourished under Ptolemaic rule is given without primary evidence; underlying source not identified. — Original evidence not traceable online.
