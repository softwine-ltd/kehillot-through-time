# Unavailable sources: Masada, Israel

- Magness, Jodi, 2019. *Masada: From Jewish Revolt to Modern Myth.* Princeton University Press. https://press.princeton.edu/books/paperback/9780691216775/masada — Main modern synthesis of the rebel occupation (housing, synagogue, miqvaot, women, how many people lived there, whether Josephus' 967 is plausible). An unreliable secondary site attributes to her an estimate of 600-700 occupants; this could not be checked. — Paywalled book; only reviews read.
- Magness, Jodi. "Women at Masada." Jewish Women's Archive Encyclopedia. https://jwa.org/encyclopedia/article/women-at-masada — A search snippet says excavations show "hundreds of Jews were living on the mountain at the time of the siege" and that about 110 loom weights came almost entirely from rebel-inhabited casemate rooms. — Page came back empty to WebFetch twice; a curl download was refused by the session's permission checks.
- Liberman, Tehila and Eyal Regev, 2021. "Masada's Rebels: Elitism, Feasts and Social Structure in Light of the Pottery and Other Findings." *Cathedra* 178: 9-34 (Hebrew). — A search snippet says the full text gives counts of inhabited rooms: 94 of 128 casemate rooms, and 110 of 289 rooms in the centre, citing Netzer. These counts could support a household-based population estimate. — Only the abstract was read (cris.iucc.ac.il); the orion page returned 503.
- (Possibly the English version of the above) "Feasting before the War: Social Structure and Organization of Masada's Rebels." https://www.researchgate.net/publication/356611355 — Same room and household data. — ResearchGate 403.
- Netzer, Ehud, 1991. *Masada III: The Yigael Yadin Excavations 1963-1965, Final Reports. The Buildings: Stratigraphy and Architecture.* Israel Exploration Society. — The architectural basis for the rebels' dwellings (casemates, subdivided palaces, shacks) and for any capacity or household estimate. — Not online.
- Yadin, Yigael and Joseph Naveh; Meshorer, Ya'akov, 1989. *Masada I: The Aramaic and Hebrew Ostraca and Jar Inscriptions; The Coins of Masada.* Israel Exploration Society. — Gives the ostraca with personal names and priestly-tithe labels, and the numbers of Hasmonean (Jannaeus) and revolt coins. — Not online.
- Talmon, Shemaryahu and Yigael Yadin, 1999. *Masada VI: Hebrew Fragments from Masada.* Israel Exploration Society. — Gives the scrolls (Ben Sira, Songs of the Sabbath Sacrifice, Joshua Apocryphon) as evidence for the community. — Not online.
- Bar-Nathan, Rachel, 2006. *Masada VII: The Pottery of Masada.* Israel Exploration Society. — Pottery evidence for the Hasmonean, Herodian and revolt phases. — Not online (only a BAS review page listed).
- Atkinson, Kenneth, 2007. "Noble Deaths at Gamla and Masada? A Critical Assessment of Josephus' Accounts of Jewish Resistance in Light of Archaeological Discoveries." In Z. Rodgers (ed.), *Making History: Josephus and Historical Method*, Brill. — Weighs Josephus' 960/967 figure against the archaeology. One unreliable site attributes to Atkinson (and to Netzer) a population of about 200-300. — Paywalled (Brill).
- Ben-Yehuda, Nachman, 1995. *The Masada Myth: Collective Memory and Mythmaking in Israel.* University of Wisconsin Press. — Critique of the 960 figure and of the skeletal evidence. — Book not online; only summaries read.
- Cotton, Hannah M., 1989. "The Date of the Fall of Masada: The Evidence of the Masada Papyri." *ZPE* 78: 157-162; and Campbell, Duncan B., 1988. "Dating the Siege of Masada." *ZPE* 73: 156-158. — Basis for dating the fall to 74 CE rather than 73 CE. — Not fetched (cited on Wikipedia).
- Stiebel, Guy et al., Tel Aviv University renewed excavations at Masada (2017 onward), field reports. — New data on the First Revolt "refugee camp" dwellings and the make-up of the population. — No excavation report found online; only press and a TAU student page.
- "Traces of Conflict: An Archaeological Perspective on the Hasmonean-Herodian Transition (63-37 BCE)." *Electrum* (ejournals.eu). https://ejournals.eu/en/journal/electrum/article/traces-of-conflict-an-archaeological-perspective-on-the-hasmonean-herodian-transition-63-37-bce — May discuss Masada during the siege by Antigonus (40-39 BCE). — Returned 503.
- Unverified secondary claims: historyrise.com ("The historical debate over the number of defenders at Masada") credits a 200-300 capacity for the casemate rooms to Yadin's team, about 200-300 people to Atkinson and Netzer, 400-500 people to a food-storage analysis, and 600-700 to Magness. None of these is referenced to a specific publication. The page reads as unsourced or AI-written, so none of these figures was entered as Population.
