# Unavailable sources: Emmaus (Emmaus Nicopolis, Imwas), Israel

- Gichon, M., 1993. "Emmaus." In E. Stern (ed.), The New Encyclopedia of Archaeological Excavations in the Holy Land, vol. 2. Jerusalem: IES/Carta. — Main archaeological and historical survey of the site (toparchy capital, Athronges, Fifth Legion tombstones); may detail Hellenistic/Early Roman remains and Jewish finds. — Not online; known only through quotations on deadseaquake.info.
- Fischer, M. and Tal, O., 2025. Khirbet el-ʿAqd: A Hellenistic-Roman Stronghold in Western Judea. Horizons / TAU Archaeological Series 6. Tel Aviv University. — Final report on the Bacchides-period stronghold 2 km SE of Emmaus; pottery, coins and stratigraphy for 2nd c. BCE to 70 CE, possibly Jewish (Hasmonean) finds. — Only the publisher's two-page flyer is online; full book not accessed.
- Fleckenstein, K.-H., Louhivuori, M. and Riesner, R., 2003. Emmaus in Judäa: Geschichte, Exegese, Archäologie. Giessen/Basel: Brunnen. — Excavations at Emmaus-Nicopolis 1993-2002, inscriptions and coins; likely holds evidence for Second Temple occupation and Jewish tombs. — Not online (book; known from library catalogue and search listings).
- Fleckenstein, K.-H. and Fleckenstein, L., 2010. Emmaus-Nicopolis: Ausgrabungen 2001-2005. — Excavation report; possibly Early Roman / Jewish remains beneath the basilica. — Not online.
- Shanks, H., 2008. "Emmaus: Where Christ Appeared." Biblical Archaeology Review 34:2, pp. 41-50. — Discusses Emmaus-Nicopolis, its Herodian Jewish kokhim tombs and ossuaries, and its mixed population. — Paywalled (BAS Library); only an image caption and the opening paragraph were readable.
- Gichon, M., Israel Exploration Journal articles (1979, 1984) on Emmaus/its Roman bath. — Possibly date the Roman structures relative to the 68 CE legionary camp. — Known only from a search listing of the HUJI corpus bibliography; not accessed (paywall/not online).
- Sharon, M., 1997. Corpus Inscriptionum Arabicarum Palaestinae, vol. 1, p. 79 (Brill). — Cited by Wikipedia for Emmaus replacing Gezer as head of a toparchy in 47 BCE and for the 68 CE legionary camp. — Paywalled; original not checked.
- Vincent, L.-H. and Abel, F.-M., 1932. Emmaüs, sa basilique et son histoire. Paris: Leroux. — Classic study of the site including early tombs and history before 70 CE. — Not online in readable form; not accessed.
- Hamichlol (Hebrew encyclopedia), "אמאוס ניקופוליס". — Hebrew summary reportedly stating the town became Jewish and was a toparchy capital in the 1st c. CE. — 403 Forbidden on fetch.
