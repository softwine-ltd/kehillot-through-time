- Trebilco, Paul R., 1991. Jewish Communities in Asia Minor. SNTS Monograph Series 69, Cambridge University Press, esp. pp. 85-103. — Revised book version of the Durham thesis (which was read); chapter on Apamea (Flaccus gold, Noah coins, Sibylline Oracles, inscriptions). — Not online (book; only cited by other pages).
- Marshall, A.J., 1975. "Flaccus and the Jews of Asia (Cicero Pro Flacco 28.67-69)." Phoenix 29.2, 139-154. https://www.jstor.org/stable/1087696 — Converts the c. 100 pounds of gold seized at Apamea into c. 135,000 drachmae and discusses the conventus-wide collection and implied Jewish population. — JSTOR paywall.
- Reinach, Théodore, 1895. Textes d'auteurs grecs et romains relatifs au judaïsme, p. 240 n. 68; and Les monnaies juives, pp. 72 f. — Calculates the Apamea gold as c. 50,000 half-shekels / over 75,000 drachmae. — Not online in accessible form (known only via Ramsay and Trebilco).
- Babelon, E., 1891. Mélanges numismatiques I, p. 169 (also "La tradition phrygienne du déluge"). — Infers that the population of Roman Apamea was largely Jewish. — Not accessed (cited by Ramsay).
- Ramsay, W.M., 1897. The Cities and Bishoprics of Phrygia I.2, ch. XV, pp. 667-669 (Heidelberg digital edition, https://digi.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/diglit/ramsay1897bd2/0336). — Jews in Apameia. — Heidelberg pages blocked (403/Anubis); the same text was read in the archive.org OCR copy instead.
- Collins, J.J., 1983. "Sibylline Oracles" in J.H. Charlesworth (ed.), Old Testament Pseudepigrapha I. — Dating (30 BCE-70 CE) and Phrygian/Apamean provenance of the Jewish substratum of Sib. Or. 1-2. — Not online (cited by Trebilco).
- Stern, M., 1974. Greek and Latin Authors on Jews and Judaism I, no. 68 (Cicero, Pro Flacco). — Commentary on the Flaccus seizure at Apamea. — Not online.
- Thonemann, Peter, 2011. The Maeander Valley: A Historical Geography from Antiochus III to Diocletian. Cambridge University Press. — Hellenistic and Roman Apameia-Kelainai; possibly the Antiochus III Jewish settlement. — Paywalled book, not accessed.
- Summerer, L., Ivantchik, A., von Kienlin, A. (eds.), 2011/2016. Kelainai-Apameia Kibotos I-II (Ausonius). — Excavation/survey results for the city; could hold urban-area data or Jewish epigraphy. — Not online / not accessed.
- Collar, Anna, 2013. Religious Networks in the Roman Empire. Cambridge University Press, p. 184. — 'Law of the Jews' heroön inscription at Apamea (3rd c. CE, outside the period). — Paywalled.
- Hogarth, D.G., 1911. "Apamea" (2), Encyclopaedia Britannica 11th ed., vol. 2, p. 159. — Source of the statement that trade at Apamea was carried on by resident Italians and Jews. — Not fetched (cited by Wikipedia).
- Kraabel, A.T., 1968. Judaism in Western Asia Minor under the Roman Empire (Harvard Th.D. thesis). — Discussion of the Flaccus seizure and Phrygian Jewry. — Not online.
